AResearch · 2024–present

Signer-Independent Sign Recognition

From 250 isolated signs toward continuous signing, tested on people and cameras the model has never seen
Key contributions
01 Found why sign models fail on new people. The model scored 97% on people it trained on but 68% on new ones (7 folds grouped by signer), a 29-point gap that a bigger model and longer training didn’t close. The limit was the variety of signers in the training data, not the model, and that shaped everything after.
02 Extended it toward phrases. A CTC model over 250 signs learns from phrases stitched together from single-sign clips in three public datasets. On single signs from 11 Deaf signers in a dataset it never saw (ASL Citizen), it is right 73.2% of the time; real continuous signing isn’t validated yet. It runs on the user’s device, exported to ONNX with outputs checked against PyTorch.
03 Checked the data before trusting the numbers. An audit found hand-tracking errors in 22% of clips and a dataset that counted the same hand twice. I rebuilt every dataset under one validated data contract and compared models against criteria set before the results, rejecting two candidates that failed them (one doubled each signer’s recordings, with no measurable gain).
